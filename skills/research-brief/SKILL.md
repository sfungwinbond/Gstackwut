# Research Brief

Build a decision aid, not a link dump.

## Workflow

1. Restate the decision, audience, time horizon, and constraints in one paragraph.
2. Separate facts to verify from judgments to make. Browse when facts may have changed or when direct attribution matters.
3. Prefer primary sources: official documentation, standards, filings, datasets, papers, and first-party announcements. Use secondary sources to add context or challenge a claim.
4. Capture each material claim with its source, publication date, event date, and confidence. Read `references/source-quality.md` when sources disagree or the topic is high stakes.
5. Reconcile conflicts explicitly. Distinguish reported fact, calculation, inference, and recommendation.
6. Deliver the answer first, then evidence, alternatives, risks, and open questions. Put links beside the claims they support.

## Quality Gate

- Verify every current number and named officeholder, version, price, policy, or schedule.
- Never invent a quote, citation, publication date, or source title.
- Keep verbatim quotations short and necessary.
- State the cutoff date and any material data gaps.
- Make the recommendation traceable to evidence and named assumptions.
