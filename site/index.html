Your first hour with a new Mac

If you can copy and paste, you can start.

Terminal is an app already on your Mac. You do not need to know how to code. Follow these four steps once, then use normal language.

Step zero · find Terminal

Open the app that sets up the rest.

Press Command-Space, type Terminal, then press Return. A small window with text appears. That is exactly where you should be.

⌘+Spacethen type Terminal
  1. 01

    Paste one command

    Use the copy button below, click the Terminal window, press ⌘ V, then press Return. The installer explains what it is doing.

  2. 02

    Let the first setup finish

    The full workbench downloads many tools, so keep the Mac awake. If macOS asks for your password, type it and press Return. Terminal shows no dots while you type; that is normal.

  3. 03

    Check that it worked

    Close Terminal, open it again, type wut doctor, and press Return. A healthy setup reports the tools and both specialist folders.

  4. 04

    Open your AI helper

    Type codex or claude, press Return, and follow its sign-in steps. Then try a real job:

    “Make a simple monthly cash-flow workbook for my business.” “Turn these meeting notes into a one-page client proposal.” “Build a short presentation explaining this plan.”

The honest boundary

WutPack is an AI-first business workbench, not a pixel-for-pixel clone of Microsoft Office. It installs LibreOffice and specialist tools, produces standard editable formats, and validates common outputs. Advanced macros, tracked changes, and proprietary extensions may still require the original application.

Straight answers

Before you put it on a business Mac.

WutPack is intentionally transparent: readable manifests, open skills, explicit updates, and no silent account setup.

Do I need to be a programmer?

No. You need to be comfortable opening Terminal, copying one command, and following on-screen prompts. After setup, you can ask for business work in normal language. The source remains visible for anyone who wants to inspect it first.

Why does Terminal ask for my Mac password?

A new Mac may need Apple’s command-line tools or Homebrew before WutPack can install applications. macOS may request an administrator password. Nothing appears while you type the password in Terminal; press Return when finished. On a company-managed Mac, ask your administrator before installing software.

What exactly is free?

WutPack’s installer, skills, and code are MIT licensed at no charge. Many installed tools are open source. Codex, Claude Code, model subscriptions, APIs, and optional third-party services have their own terms and may cost money.

Does it replace Microsoft Office?

It covers a large share of everyday small-business creation and analysis through LibreOffice and specialist libraries, with standard Office-compatible outputs. It does not promise full fidelity for every macro, tracked-change workflow, or proprietary extension.

Does it work on Apple silicon and Intel Macs?

Yes. Setup detects both Homebrew locations, and the repository runs architecture-specific clean-install jobs for Apple silicon and Intel macOS runners.

Does WutPack read or upload my business data?

WutPack does not collect credentials or silently configure accounts. Your chosen AI host and any services you enable have their own data policies, so review those before using sensitive material.

Why not install these tools ad hoc?

Ad hoc installs drift and disappear between environments. WutPack keeps a known toolset, twelve routed workflows, deterministic helpers, and output validation available across terminals and agent sessions.

What platforms are supported?

The bootstrapper currently supports macOS. Both Apple silicon and Intel are covered. Windows packaging is intentionally out of scope for this release.
